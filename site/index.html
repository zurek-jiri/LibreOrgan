Pipe organ sampler for creative minds

Design your console. Build your stop list. Perform.

OrganSampler plays sampled pipe organs from plain folders of WAV files - one folder per stop, its pipes as WAV files - and turns any number of such folders into an organ of your own design.

No organ definition file, no installer, no account. Load the folders, let the program propose the organ, shape it - divisions, stops, perspectives, audio routes, voicing - and save it as a configuration. Free software under the GNU GPL v3 for Windows, macOS and Linux.

Inside the program

From a folder of samples to an organ.

Four windows do the work: the console, the Stop List where the organ is built, the Divisions dialog with the audio routes, and the Configuration Editor with the manual one click away from every option.

01

Build the organ yourself - from any sources

Load a folder tree of samples; the program recognises the stops, their footages and pitches from the files, the microphone perspectives from the folder prefixes, and proposes the divisions. Mix stops of different sample folders and producers into one console, move them between divisions, duplicate them as transmissions and extensions (a 16' from an 8' by transposition, a mutation from a principal), merge stops, merge a second configuration into the organ, arrange the grid. A Hauptwerk organ definition opens as a configuration as well. This is what sets OrganSampler apart.

02

Every route on any sound card

Every perspective of every division is an audio route with its own device, buffer size and output channels - ASIO and Windows Audio side by side, several interfaces at once, an aggregated device for the rest. One IR reverb per route, mixed in parallel on the render workers. A measurement button finds the polyphony your computer reaches with this very organ.

03

Voice every pipe

Per-key volume, pitch and tuning errors, celeste detection, temperaments (historical and your own), velocity layers, round-robin variants, a wind model with pressure drops and oscillation, tremulants per division, swell boxes, a general crescendo, combination banks, MIDI learn on every control.

04

Missing pipes, filled

A rank with gaps, or a stop that exists only as an 8', is completed by pitch-shifted copies of the neighbouring pipes - several transposition methods, rebuilt offline into a cache, so that nothing is interpolated on the fly and nothing is altered silently.

05

Fast, measured, honest

The sound engine renders on all cores with a lock-free control thread; the polyphony display shows the real peak, the DSP field the busiest audio callback. Every start, device, load and crash is written to an activity log, and "Report a problem" in the Configuration Editor turns it into a bug report with one click.

06

The manual lives inside

The complete user manual is embedded: the question mark beside every option of the Configuration Editor opens the chapter that explains it, chapter names in the text are links, and a Find box searches it. Nothing is read from beside the executable; one file is the whole program.

07

Light on RAM, CPU and disk

On a modest computer the saving options of the load box squeeze an 80 GB sample set into 8 GB of RAM, and the samples are read straight from their packed archives - nothing to unpack, no second copy on the disk. On a strong computer the same program reaches up to 500 000 pipes of polyphony; it measures what your machine can do and sets its limit itself.

What you need: sample folders (below), an audio interface (ASIO for the lowest latency on Windows; CoreAudio on macOS; ALSA or JACK on Linux), a MIDI keyboard or an organ console with MIDI out. RAM for the samples: a large wet organ with several perspectives needs tens of gigabytes; a small dry one runs on a laptop. The saving options of the load box bring a large organ down to a tenth of its RAM.

Download

One file. Put it anywhere.

OrganSampler is one executable: no installer, nothing written beside it. Each build comes as the archive native to its system - unpack it and start the program. Settings, voicing, combinations and the cache of filled pipes live in your application-data folder. The window title shows the version.

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit: OrganSampler-windows.zip (OrganSampler.exe inside) - ASIO, Windows Audio (WASAPI) and the aggregated WASAPI device. The executable is not signed: Windows may ask once ("More info" → "Run anyway").
  • macOS 11 Big Sur or newer, Apple silicon: OrganSampler-macos.zip; Intel Macs, macOS 10.11 El Capitan or newer: OrganSampler-macos-10.11-intel.zip - CoreAudio. Not notarised: open the app the first time with right-click → Open. Both untested by the project so far.
  • Linux x86-64, glibc 2.38 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04, Mint 22, Debian 13, Fedora 39 and newer): OrganSampler-linux-x86_64.tar.gz (tar xzf; the executable bit is kept) - ALSA or JACK (PipeWire).

The macOS and Linux builds are compiled and self-tested by the project's continuous integration; they are young and less used than the Windows build - please report what you find.

Samples

Folders of WAV files - that is all.

OrganSampler has no format of its own and needs no organ definition file. What it plays is a tree of folders: a top folder holding one folder per stop, each stop folder holding the WAVs of its pipes, named after their MIDI keys (036-C.wav, 037-C#.wav ...; several files for one key play in turn), with the releases in a rel subfolder and the microphone perspectives as prefixes (dir-, dif-, rear-). Loop points and the recorded pitch come from the WAV's smpl chunk; from the pitch the program learns the footage, the tuning and the temperament of every rank. In OrganSampler the words "sample set" keep their original, plain meaning: a bunch of WAV samples organised in folders - not a packaged virtual organ with a definition file.

Organ Samples                 <- the top folder you load
├── Principal 8
│   ├── 036-C.wav             <- a WAV per pipe, named after its key (several for one key play in turn)
│   ├── 037-C#.wav
│   └── rel                   <- the releases, named like the attacks
├── dir-Trompete 8            <- the "Direct" perspective of Trompete 8
├── dif-Trompete 8            <- the "Diffuse" perspective
└── Mixtura

Where the samples come from

  • OrganSampler ships no samples and never will: they are bought or downloaded elsewhere and stay the property of their authors.
  • Sonus Paradisi - sample folders in wave format usually work with OrganSampler as they are. The Stralsund, St. Jakobi, Wegscheider organ is the instrument the program grew up with.

Try it at once: Rural Organ

  • The demo organ - a small village organ, three divisions, 24 stops, dry recordings with IR reverb: the room comes from the two impulse responses the configuration puts on its routes. The one package that carries samples, with its portable configuration. Download RuralOrgan.rar (about 700 MB), start OrganSampler, Load Configuration and choose the .rar itself: the samples are read from the archive, nothing to unpack (unpacked, it loads faster from a fast disk). Samples by Sonus Paradisi, free of charge to try the program.
  • More free material: Sonus Paradisi's Free stuff page.

Your own recordings

  • Record the pipes, name the files after the keys, put them in a folder per stop. Loop points and the pitch belong ideally in the WAV's smpl chunk (any audio editor writes it), but their absence is no obstacle: the program copes without them, and missing pipes are filled by transposition.
  • The exact rules: chapter "How the sample folders must be organised" of the manual.

Configurations, Organs

Someone kind has done the work for you.

A configuration is a complete organ built in OrganSampler - stop list, voicing, audio routes, couplers, combinations, crescendo - saved as one .json file. With a published configuration, a player who owns the same sample folders loads the whole organ in one step. The samples themselves are never published; a configuration only names the sample folders it needs, and the repository refuses anything else in its configurations folder. The samples are bought or downloaded elsewhere - from Sonus Paradisi above all.

Using one

  1. Find the organ in the list of published configurations (the link below) and open its README: it says which sample folders it needs and where from, and its Download link gives the zipped .json.
  2. Load Configuration in the top bar and choose the downloaded zip as it is: the .json inside is read and copied into your configurations folder. Where the download lies does not matter.
  3. The sample folders are found through your sample locations - the folders above your libraries, which the program remembers by itself. On a new computer a division may show "Sample folder not found": click it and choose any folder of the set - the tree, the folder above it or a neighbouring tree. The program finds the tree from there, the other missing ones around it, loads them all and remembers the place for the next configuration.

Publishing yours

  1. Save it with "Portable configuration (to share with others)" ticked: no MIDI devices, no audio devices, nothing of your computer - the organ only.
  2. The program asks once for the organ, the producer of the samples, the link to their page and your name, and writes the export into the Publish folder of your configurations folder: the portable .json, the zip for the upload (the configuration and its README inside) and the README beside it for reading.
  3. Press "Publish on GitHub" in the window that follows: the form opens with the title and the organ filled in, the Publish folder beside it. Attach the zip and submit. A robot takes the README out of it and checks them (a real organ configuration, saved with the organ loaded, and the README written for it) and proposes them as a pull request; after the merge the organ gets its folder and its line in the list by itself. A new version sent the same way replaces the old one; the "Remove my configuration" form takes it off the list. Only the zip: no sample, no impulse response, nothing else is accepted, ever.

Problems and ideas

A report with the log is a report that gets fixed.

OrganSampler writes an activity log of every start, device, load and crash. A bug report that carries it is usually fixed in the next release; one without it often cannot be.

Reporting a bug
  1. In the program: Configuration Editor → Report a problem... Describe what happened and how to reproduce it; the program bundles its activity log, crash reports, settings, the last self-test and a description of the computer and the audio devices into one zip, takes screenshots of its own windows (recommended - keep the dialog in question open), and lets you add screenshot files of your own.
  2. It then opens the GitHub bug report in your browser with the fields filled in and the report folder (in the program's application-data folder) beside it: drag the zip into "Logs", the pictures into "Screenshots", and submit. A free GitHub account is needed to submit.
